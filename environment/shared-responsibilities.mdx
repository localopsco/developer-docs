title: Shared responsibilities
description:
  Who owns what in a LocalOps environment - your team, LocalOps, and your cloud provider - across apps, infrastructure
  and security.
---

A LocalOps environment runs your services, on infrastructure LocalOps provisions and configures, inside a cloud account
you own. Each of the three parties guards a different layer.

In short:

- **Your team** owns your services and their uptime, and governs the cloud account they run in.
- **LocalOps** provisions the environment, configures it securely, and keeps deployments and the observability stack
  running.
- **Your cloud provider** runs the physical infrastructure and the managed services everything is built on.

## What your team owns

### Your applications

- **Code and dependencies.** Bugs, vulnerable libraries, and application-level authentication and authorization are
  yours to fix. LocalOps controls who can reach a service over the network, not what the service does once a request
  arrives.
- **Container images.** What runs inside your container is defined by your Dockerfile - base image, OS packages, the
  user it runs as, and which capabilities it needs. Keep base images patched, and run as a non-root user where you can.
- **Uptime of your services.** Crashes, memory leaks, slow queries and failing health checks in your app are yours to
  debug. LocalOps gives you the [logs](/environment/monitoring/logs), [metrics](/environment/monitoring/metrics) and
  [alerts](/environment/monitoring/alerts) to do it.
- **Health checks and resources.** You define [health checks](/environment/services/ops-json) and set CPU and memory for
  each service, so Kubernetes knows when to restart a container and how much room it has.
- **Data inside your services.** What you write to databases, caches, queues and buckets, its schema and migrations, and
  how long you keep it.

### Your secrets and configuration

- **Secret values.** You decide what goes into [secrets](/environment/services/secrets) and when to rotate them, such as
  third-party API keys. LocalOps stores and delivers them securely.
- **Sharing between services.** A service only receives another service's secrets when you import them. Keep imports to
  what each service needs.
- **`ops.json` choices.** Settings that loosen defaults are your call: making a database publicly accessible,
  [privileged containers](/environment/services/ops-json#privileged-containers), or
  [SSL passthrough](/environment/services/ops-json#ssl-passthrough) (where your app then owns its own certificate).

### Your cloud account

The environment lives in your account, so account-level governance stays with you:

- **Cloud account guardrails.** AWS Organizations, service control policies, GuardDuty, Security Hub, AWS Config and
  budgets are yours to set up. Account-wide defaults, like EBS encryption by default, apply on top of what LocalOps
  configures.
- **CloudTrail.** Every action LocalOps takes in your account is recorded by CloudTrail. Keeping it enabled and retained
  is up to you.
- **The connection role.** You create the role LocalOps uses with CloudFormation, and you can revoke it at any time by
  deleting the stack.
- **Quotas and cost.** Service quotas, reserved capacity and the cloud bill for the resources in your environments.

### Your network edge

- **Domains and DNS.** You own your domains, and point [custom domains](/environment/services/custom-domain) at your
  environment.
- **Who can reach a web service.** Per-service IP allowlists, and a WAF like Cloudflare in front of your services, are
  yours to configure. See
  [Web application firewall with Cloudflare](/environment/security#web-application-firewall-with-cloudflare).
- **Connecting other networks.** VPC peering, Transit Gateway or VPN links to other networks, along with the routes and
  security group rules they need. Pick a custom CIDR when you create an environment if you plan to peer it.

### Your people

- **LocalOps account governance.** Who you invite to LocalOps, what [role](/team/understanding-roles) they get, and
  removing people who leave.
- **Cloud access.** Who in your organisation has direct access to your AWS account outside LocalOps.
- **GitHub / GitLab account.** Who can push to the repositories and branches LocalOps deploys from, and which
  repositories you give LocalOps access to when you connect [GitHub](/accounts/connect-github) or
  [GitLab](/accounts/connect-gitlab).
- **Privileged pod approval.** Allowing privileged containers on a cloud connection is an admin decision you make.

## What LocalOps owns

LocalOps provisions and configures everything between your code and the cloud provider, and keeps it working. See
Environment security controls on [AWS](/environment/security) and [GCP](/environment/security-gcp) for the full list of
what's enabled.

- **Environment provisioning.** A dedicated VPC and Kubernetes cluster for every environment, with subnets, NAT, load
  balancer, ingress, certificates, monitoring and backups set up from the start.
- **Secure defaults.** Private subnets, VPC-only security groups for data services, TLS on every web service, encryption
  at rest, a per-cluster KMS key, and control-plane audit logging.
- **Least-privilege access.** Keyless, short-lived access to your account, and per-component IAM roles. Your services'
  role starts empty and only gains access to the dependencies you declare.
- **Cloud resources you declare.** Databases, caches, queues and buckets from `ops.json`, created with encryption,
  backups and locked-down network access.
- **Automated deployment pipelines.** Building your images on every push, rolling them out, and waiting for them to
  become healthy - one service at a time, without touching the others.
- **Developer experience.** The console, [CLI](/cli/usage), [preview environments](/use-cases/ephemeral) for pull
  requests, and built-in logs and metrics, so your team ships without writing infrastructure code.
- **Secrets delivery.** Storing secrets in your account and injecting them into the right containers, and only those.
- **SSL certificates.** Issuing and renewing SSL/TLS certificates for every web service and custom domain.
- **Auto-scaling.** Adding and removing replicas of your services on CPU, memory and schedules you set in
  [`ops.json`](/environment/services/autoscaling).
- **Observability stack.** Running and keeping available the logs, metrics, dashboards and alerts in each environment.
- **Backups set-up.** Backup tooling in each environment, and automated backups for the databases and caches it creates.

## What your cloud provider owns

- **Physical infrastructure.** Data centers, hardware, networking and the virtualization layer.
- **Compute uptime.** Availability of the virtual machines your nodes run on, and replacing failed underlying hosts.
- **Managed services.** Availability and durability of the managed services your environment uses, such as EKS, EC2,
  RDS, ElastiCache, S3, KMS and STS on AWS.
- **Service-level guarantees.** Uptime and durability as defined in each service's SLA.

## Who owns what, at a glance

| Area                          | Your team                                         | LocalOps                                                                            | Cloud provider                         |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Application                   | Code, dependencies, app-level auth, bugs          | -                                                                                   | -                                      |
| Containers                    | Image contents, base image patching, runtime user | Running them on the cluster, privileged off by default                              | -                                      |
| Image building and deployment | Dockerfile, pushing to the branch you deploy from | Build infrastructure, building images on every push, storing them, rolling them out | Container registry                     |
| Service uptime                | Crashes, health checks, resource sizing           | Rollouts, restarts on failed health checks                                          | -                                      |
| Scaling                       | Floors, ceilings and targets in `ops.json`        | Auto-scaling replicas to match load                                                 | Capacity for new nodes                 |
| Kubernetes cluster            | -                                                 | Configuration, RBAC, namespaces, add-ons                                            | Managed control plane                  |
| Network                       | Peering, IP allowlists, WAF, DNS for your domains | VPC, subnets, NAT, load balancer, security groups                                   | Underlying network                     |
| Encryption                    | Your app's own TLS, if using SSL passthrough      | TLS certificates, encryption at rest, KMS keys                                      | Encryption services (KMS)              |
| Identity and access           | Team members and roles, direct cloud access       | Keyless connection, per-component IAM roles                                         | IAM and STS                            |
| Secrets                       | Values, rotation of your own keys                 | Storage in your account, delivery to the right containers                           | Parameter Store, Secrets Manager       |
| Data                          | What you store, schema, retention                 | Encrypted, backed-up databases and caches                                           | Durability of storage                  |
| Backups                       | Restores and deciding what to restore             | Backup tooling and automated database backups                                       | Snapshot and backup storage            |
| Monitoring                    | Acting on alerts from your services               | Logs, metrics, dashboards and alerts stack                                          | CloudWatch, for control-plane logs     |
| Audit                         | Keeping CloudTrail enabled and retained           | Every action under a named, auditable session                                       | Recording API calls                    |
| Cloud account governance      | Guardrails, quotas, cost                          | -                                                                                   | -                                      |
| LocalOps account governance   | Members, roles, cloud connections, offboarding    | -                                                                                   | -                                      |
| GitHub / GitLab account       | Repo access, branch protection, who can push      | Deploying from the repos you connect                                                | -                                      |
| Physical infrastructure       | -                                                 | -                                                                                   | Data centers, hardware, compute uptime |
